# Interactive audio story research

**Status:** Research complete; use as interaction and content guidance for the first story.

## Candidates reviewed

### Lunii / FLAM

Lunii positions its products as interactive audio experiences for children. FLAM is aimed at roughly ages 7–12 and describes collecting items, earning rewards, choosing character traits, and exploring fantastic universes. Lunii’s broader storyteller catalog emphasizes composing stories and listening without a screen.

**Useful pattern:** choices and collected state can be carried through audio without turning the product into a text-heavy game.

### Yoto

Yoto describes a screen-free audio platform with stories, music, activities, podcasts, and kid-controlled physical interactions. Its creator examples show short stories with character voices, sound effects, and environmental sound beds.

**Useful pattern:** short, production-focused audio chapters and a clear separation between narration, effects, and ambient sound make the experience replayable and understandable without visuals.

### TWIST Tales

TWIST Tales presents interactive audiobooks in which listener choices shape the path, with high-quality voice acting and soundscapes.

**Useful pattern:** frame the listener as an active participant, but keep the choice set small enough to understand while brushing.

## Product implications

- Use a data-driven scene graph with short clips, two or three choices at most, and deterministic outcomes.
- Let brushing progress unlock or color the journey, while explicit choices determine the child’s agency. This avoids making the child feel that an imperfect camera score “ruined” the story.
- Use a narrator plus a small recurring cast, consistent sound motifs, and short ambient loops. Avoid requiring visual reading during brushing.
- Provide a parent-readable transcript and captions, but make audio the canonical experience.
- For v0, ship one polished 4–6 minute story assembled from clips and a few endings rather than a large procedural story system.

## Implication for BrushTales

Use a small authored scene graph for v0: short narration clips, two or three explicit choices, and brushing progress as a gentle modifier of the route or ending. The story must remain coherent and enjoyable when the camera is disabled.
